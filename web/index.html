Γ 03Price and gamma

How gamma moves with price

Every 5 minutes the pipeline stores a new snapshot. The amber line is the SPY price. The bars are net gamma at the same market time.

When net gamma is positive, dealers sell rallies and buy dips, so moves tend to shrink. When it is negative, they do the opposite, so moves tend to grow.

Γ 04Pipeline

From CBOE to this page

Five KNIME workflows do the work, run headless from the command line. Extract pulls the full SPY option chain and the price. Transform parses about 12,700 contracts and keeps the ones with open interest and gamma.

Compute sums gamma per strike into a SQLite database. Export writes the small files this page reads, as JSON, CSV and XML.

Γ 05Formula

One line of maths

For each contract, gamma times open interest is how much the dealers' delta changes when SPY moves $1. Times 100 shares per contract gives shares. Times spot squared and 1% gives dollars of hedging per 1% move.

Calls count as positive and puts as negative. The sum over all contracts is net GEX.

Γ 06Assumptions

What the number assumes

Open interest does not say who holds an option. So GEX needs a guess about the other side of each trade. This page uses the common one: customers buy puts and sell calls, so dealers are long calls and short puts.

The guess is wrong on some days. Read GEX as a rough map of hedging pressure, not as a forecast.

Γ 07Questions

Frequently asked

Short answers to the questions people ask first.

What is gamma?

Delta is how much an option's price moves when the stock moves $1. Gamma is how fast delta changes. It is highest for options with a strike near the price and close to expiry.

What does a GEX of – mean?

For every 1% move in SPY, dealers must trade about that many dollars of SPY to stay hedged. When the number is negative, they trade in the same direction as the move, so they push it further.

Why do deep in-the-money or out-of-the-money options have almost no gamma?

Their delta is already close to 1 or close to 0. A small move in the stock barely changes it. So they add almost nothing to GEX, even with large open interest.

Why per 1% move and not per $1?

A 1% move is a fair yardstick at any price level. The S² · 0.01 term converts gamma, which is per $1, into dollars of hedging per 1% move. It also makes GEX comparable across days.

What is Black-Scholes?

A model that prices an option from the stock price, the strike, the time to expiry, interest rates and volatility. Greeks such as gamma come out of models like it. This page uses the gamma that CBOE publishes for each contract and does not recompute it.

Why assume customers sell calls and buy puts?

Many investors buy puts as insurance and sell calls against stock they own. Dealers take the other side. It is a convention, not a fact. On some days customers buy calls in size, and the sign of GEX is then wrong.

Do 0DTE and expiry days make the data messy? Is CBOE data real at the open and close?

Yes, in part. Same-day options never reach open interest, so GEX misses them. On expiry days a large block of open interest disappears at once, so GEX jumps. CBOE quotes are real but delayed about 15 minutes, and open interest changes only once a day.

What would real positioning data cost?

Cboe Open-Close data splits volume by customer type and by opening or closing trades. End-of-day data costs about $600 a month. That is outside the scope of this project.
